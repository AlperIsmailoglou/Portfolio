### Summary

Developed as a strategic industrial design intervention for environmental crisis management, the "MEDOmi" project delivers a highly specialized product-service system engineered to optimize mass casualty triage during severe wildfires. Initial systemic research encompassed a broad spectrum of natural disasters, ultimately pivoting to address the acute logistical and medical bottlenecks experienced within the "Cold Zones" of wildfire evacuation sites. During such catastrophic events, emergency medical services face overwhelming patient volumes, delayed ambulance transport, and chaotic field conditions that severely hinder the provision of immediate, life-saving pre-hospital care.

To resolve these operational deficits, MEDOmi functions as a rapidly deployable, modular medical bed and shelter ecosystem. Engineered from aerospace-grade 6061-T6 aluminum and featuring an ABS outer shell with a TPU-coated mattress, the system guarantees maximum durability, fluid resistance, and adherence to stringent national emergency (EKAB) standards. The architecture incorporates an adjustable backrest, an integrated telescopic IV pole, and a slide-out auxiliary table for medical instrumentation, empowering paramedics to stabilize patients efficiently. Designed for extreme portability, the MEDOmi units can be compactly folded, easily transported by commandeered civilian or municipal vehicles, and rapidly assembled on-site, effectively transforming any designated safe zone into a highly organized, fully functional temporary field hospital.

### Disaster Risk Analysis and Contextual Research

The preliminary investigative phase of this project required a macroscopic analysis of various natural disasters to identify overarching vulnerabilities within existing emergency management frameworks. This foundational research was imperative for understanding the holistic nature of disaster risk reduction and the complex physiological and psychological traumas inflicted upon affected populations.

The initial analysis examined the multifaceted nature of catastrophic flooding, encompassing coastal, fluvial, pluvial, and groundwater inundation. Research indicated that floods fundamentally disrupt urban ecosystems, destroying critical infrastructure and causing severe displacement. Beyond the immediate threat of drowning, which accounts for the vast majority of flood-related fatalities, survivors frequently suffer from severe secondary health crises. Focus group data and interviews from historical flood events revealed widespread incidences of respiratory infections, gastrointestinal diseases from contaminated water, and profound psychological trauma, including acute stress disorders and adjustment disorders. The logistical paralysis caused by flooded roadways drastically impedes the deployment of traditional emergency vehicles, forcing reliance on aerial assets or specialized watercraft, thereby delaying critical medical interventions.

Parallel investigations into seismic catastrophes further illuminated the limitations of conventional emergency infrastructure. Earthquakes, categorized by magnitude on the Richter scale, inflict catastrophic structural failures, transforming urban centers into hazardous zones of debris and entrapment. The analysis of major seismic events, including those in Turkey and Syria, highlighted the critical necessity for rapid extrication and the immediate establishment of decentralized medical care. Survivors often endure prolonged periods of entrapment, leading to severe crush injuries, hypothermia, and acute dehydration. The destruction of local hospitals and the disruption of essential lifelines, such as electricity and sterile water supplies, render traditional healthcare facilities inoperable, forcing medical personnel to conduct complex triage and stabilization procedures in austere, unpredictable outdoor environments.

Despite the profound impacts of floods and earthquakes, the research trajectory deliberately converged on the escalating threat of forest wildfires. Wildfires represent a unique logistical and medical challenge due to their rapid, unpredictable propagation and the intense thermal and respiratory hazards they generate. The genesis of these fires, whether stemming from natural phenomena like lightning strikes or human factors such as negligence and infrastructure failures, inevitably leads to a rapid depletion of available emergency resources. The analysis of devastating historical fires in Greece, such as those in Ileia (2007) and Mati (2018), exposed critical systemic failures. Testimonies from survivors and first responders detailed a chaotic operational theater characterized by severe traffic congestion from abandoned vehicles, zero-visibility environments due to dense smoke, and widespread panic. Most critically, the research identified a severe bottleneck in the post-evacuation phase: the inability to provide immediate, organized medical care to victims suffering from burns, smoke inhalation, and heat exhaustion while awaiting delayed ambulance transport.

### Hot Zones, Cold Zones, and Crisis Logistics

To engineer a viable industrial solution for wildfire management, it was necessary to map the spatial and operational dynamics of the crisis environment. Emergency management protocols delineate the disaster area into distinct operational sectors to ensure the safety of both victims and responders.

The "**Hot Zone**" is defined as the area of immediate, localized danger where the fire is actively burning. Operations within this sector are strictly limited to specialized firefighting personnel utilizing heavy extinguishing equipment and aerial water drops. Medical personnel and standard emergency medical services (EMS) are strictly prohibited from entering the Hot Zone due to the extreme thermal threat, lack of oxygen, and high probability of entrapment.

Consequently, the extraction of victims leads to the "**Cold Zone.**" The Cold Zone is a designated, secured geographic location situated at a safe distance from the fire's trajectory, typically featuring direct access to main road networks to facilitate logistical movement. These areas, often large open spaces with dirt or concrete foundations, serve as the primary assembly and evacuation points for displaced civilians. It is within this specific context that the MEDOmi system was designed to operate.

The Cold Zone operates as the critical nexus between immediate rescue and definitive hospital care. However, the research identified that these zones frequently degenerate into chaotic environments. First responders, including personnel from the National Center for Emergency Assistance (EKAB), the Red Cross, and various volunteer organizations, are tasked with managing a massive influx of casualties under extreme environmental stress, including high ambient temperatures and residual smoke. The lack of pre-established, organized medical infrastructure in these open spaces severely hampers the ability of medical staff to conduct accurate triage, administer intravenous fluids, and stabilize critical patients prior to their evacuation to permanent medical facilities.

### Mass Casualty Triage and Medical Protocols

The core functional requirement of the MEDOmi system is the facilitation of effective medical triage during a Mass Casualty Incident (MCI). Triage is the systematic process of prioritizing patient care based on the severity of their injuries and the likelihood of survival, particularly when the number of victims vastly exceeds the available medical resources and personnel.

The research extensively analyzed established field triage protocols, including the Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment (START) method and the Secondary Assessment of Victim Endpoint (SAVE) system. In the chaotic environment of a wildfire Cold Zone, primary triage is conducted rapidly to categorize patients into distinct color-coded groups. The "**Red**" (Immediate) category designates patients with life-threatening injuries, such as severe airway burns or massive hemorrhage, who require immediate, life-saving intervention. The "**Yellow**" (Delayed) category includes patients with serious, but not immediately life-threatening injuries, such as stable fractures or moderate burns, who require continuous monitoring and pain management. The "**Green**" (Minor) category encompasses the "walking wounded" who require minimal intervention, while the "**Black" or "Gray**" categories designate those who are deceased or have injuries incompatible with life under austere field conditions.

The MEDOmi system was specifically architected to support the stabilization and management of patients transitioning through these triage categories, particularly those in the Red and Yellow groups. In the field, emergency medical technicians (EMTs) and paramedics face immense pressure. The pediatric demographic presents an even greater challenge, as children exhibit different physiological vital signs, require specialized equipment sizing, and are highly susceptible to emotional trauma. Furthermore, the operational philosophy of EMS units plays a crucial role. While some systems prioritize a "Scoop and Run" approach, rapidly transporting unstable patients to trauma centers, the sheer volume of casualties and compromised road networks during a wildfire often necessitate a "Stay and Play" methodology. In this scenario, medical personnel must establish advanced field hospitals to stabilize patients, secure airways, and manage shock on-site. The MEDOmi bed provides the essential physical platform required to execute these complex medical interventions safely and ergonomically outside of a traditional hospital setting.

### EKAB Specifications

To ensure the MEDOmi system possessed real-world viability and could be seamlessly integrated into national emergency response fleets, the design parameters were strictly aligned with the technical specifications mandated by the Greek National Center for Emergency Assistance (EKAB) and European CEN standards (EN 1789 and EN 1865).

The exhaustive review of EKAB procurement documents dictated the exact dimensional and functional constraints for ambulance stretchers, patient transit chairs, and mobile medical equipment. A standard ambulance stretcher must feature an adjustable backrest capable of reaching a 75-degree inclination, support a minimum dynamic load of 150 kilograms, and possess precise dimensions (approximately 1.95 meters in length and 0.55 meters in width) to ensure compatibility with ambulance loading mechanisms and internal spatial constraints. Furthermore, all medical surfaces must be constructed from non-porous, antibacterial, and anti-fungal materials capable of withstanding rigorous chemical disinfection protocols.

The MEDOmi system expanded upon these rigid specifications to create a hybrid solution: a bed that offers the clinical functionality of a hospital examination table combined with the rugged portability of a military field stretcher. The design incorporated the necessity for vital sign monitoring equipment, portable electric suction units, and advanced airway management tools by integrating localized storage and support features directly into the bed's architecture.

### The Design Brief and Constraints

Synthesizing the crisis logistics of wildfire Cold Zones with the stringent medical requirements of triage protocols, the formal design brief was established: to engineer a product-service system that provides seating, resting, and shading for casualties during the execution of triage in the cold zones of a wildfire.

The strategic objectives were multi-faceted: First, to enhance medical activity in emergencies by capitalizing on the mobilization of commandeered municipal and private vehicles for rapid logistical deployment. Second, to create a system that provides immediate physical relief and psychological comfort to trauma victims. Third, to enable the rapid provision of first aid and precise clinical assessment. Fourth, to establish an organized field structure that facilitates clear communication with receiving hospitals. Ultimately, the system aimed to drastically decongest ambulances and regional hospital emergency departments by allowing stable patients to be managed effectively in the field.

The engineering specifications required the medical bed to possess dimensions of 1.92 x 0.70 x 0.60 meters, ensuring an ergonomic working height for standing medical personnel. The torso section required an adjustable inclination mechanism ranging from 0 to 75 degrees to assist patients with respiratory distress—a common pathology in wildfire scenarios due to smoke inhalation. The structural integrity of the frame demanded a minimum weight-bearing capacity of 150 kilograms. To optimize the workflow of EMTs, the bed required an integrated, slide-out surface (minimum 25x25 cm) for the secure placement of pharmaceuticals, intubation kits, and diagnostic tools. Furthermore, the system needed a dedicated mechanism for the prominent display of triage color-coding tags to ensure rapid visual identification by coordinating officers.

Complementing the medical bed, the brief mandated a rapid-deployment shelter system to protect patients and staff from the intense summer sun and environmental debris. This shelter required robust anchoring mechanisms for dirt or concrete surfaces, utilization of waterproof and flame-retardant PU or PVC textiles, and a spatial footprint that allocated 1.4 square meters per bed, alongside a 1-meter wide central corridor to permit the unobstructed movement of medical personnel and stretcher-bearers.

### Architectural and Material Innovation

Through a rigorous iterative process—evolving from initial conceptual drafts (Concepts 1, 2, and 3) that explored various folding mechanisms, telescopic supports, and material applications—the final "MEDOmi" architecture was solidified. The MEDOmi bed is a masterpiece of specialized industrial design, harmonizing mechanical resilience with clinical precision.

**Structural Framework and Kinematics** The foundational architecture of the MEDOmi bed relies on a sophisticated folding inner skeleton. The primary frame is constructed from 6061-T6 aluminum alloy. This specific aerospace-grade material was selected for its exceptional strength-to-weight ratio, allowing the bed to easily support massive dynamic loads while remaining lightweight enough to be carried by a single emergency responder when folded. All critical joints, hinges, safety locks, and structural screws are precision-machined from high-grade stainless steel to prevent corrosion and ensure long-term mechanical reliability under austere environmental conditions.

The deployment mechanism utilizes a series of fold-out legs equipped with revolving foot joints and high-friction grip footings. This allows the bed to automatically self-level and maintain absolute stability on the uneven, rocky, or unpaved terrains characteristic of wildfire Cold Zones.

**Clinical Surfaces and Materiality** The patient-facing surfaces of the MEDOmi bed are engineered for maximum infection control and trauma support. The primary mattress is encased in Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU). TPU was strategically selected because it creates a highly elastic, extremely low-porosity surface that is completely impermeable to blood, bodily fluids, and chemical contaminants. This ensures that the bed can be rapidly sanitized using harsh hospital-grade disinfectants between patients, adhering to strict cross-contamination protocols.

Beneath the TPU mattress, the outer structural shell of the bed platform is manufactured from Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS) plastic. ABS provides a rigid, impact-resistant foundation that is crucial for performing cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), as it prevents the downward flex that would otherwise absorb the kinetic energy of chest compressions.

**Integrated Medical Affordances** Understanding that field medics operate with limited hands and space, MEDOmi integrates several crucial medical affordances directly into its chassis. A slide-in auxiliary table is seamlessly hidden within the ABS shell. When extracted, it provides a stable, sterile field for paramedics to prepare intravenous (IV) lines, organize syringes, or place a portable defibrillator (AED).

Furthermore, the bed features an integrated, telescopic IV pole that stows flat against the frame during transport. When deployed, it allows for the secure, elevated suspension of intravenous fluids and medications, which is a critical requirement for treating burn victims and patients in hypovolemic shock. The design also incorporates specific tethering points and railing systems to secure oxygen cylinders and secure the patient using standard quick-release medical restraints.

### Logistical Deployment and Systemic Usability

The true strategic value of the MEDOmi system lies in its logistical agility. The entire unit is designed to fold entirely flat into a compact, easily stackable geometric profile. This form factor is highly intentional; it allows vast quantities of MEDOmi beds to be stockpiled efficiently in regional civil protection warehouses.

When a wildfire crisis is declared, the system leverages the protocols of civilian vehicle commandeering. Because the folded beds require minimal volumetric space, they can be rapidly loaded into the cargo bays of standard municipal vans, commandeered commercial transport trucks, or even heavy-duty passenger vehicles.

Upon arrival at the designated Cold Zone, the deployment sequence is intuitively designed to require zero specialized tools and minimal cognitive load—a critical factor for personnel operating under extreme psychological stress. A single responder can carry the folded unit using the integrated ergonomic handle. The assembly process is executed in three rapid motions: unlocking the transport latch, unfolding the primary aluminum chassis, and securing the locking connectors on the fold-out legs. Within seconds, a fully functional, highly stable medical platform is established.

By organizing these units systematically beneath the accompanying modular shelter tents, emergency coordinators can instantly manifest a high-capacity field hospital. This organized spatial arrangement allows incoming triage officers to efficiently route casualties to designated beds based on their START/SAVE color codes, effectively transforming a chaotic evacuation point into a controlled, clinical triage environment.

### Conclusion

The MEDOmi project transcends traditional industrial design by delivering a vital infrastructural asset for global disaster risk reduction. By meticulously analyzing the brutal realities of wildfire crisis management and synthesizing these insights with the uncompromising standards of emergency medical care, the design offers a profound logistical advantage to first responders. The MEDOmi system ensures that victims of environmental catastrophes receive dignified, organized, and life-saving medical intervention during the most critical hours of their evacuation. Ultimately, this scalable, durable, and highly ergonomic product-service system represents a critical advancement in how modern societies prepare for, and respond to, the escalating challenges of mass casualty environmental disasters.
